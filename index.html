Build a command. Understand it.

Pick a tool, choose flags from buttons that explain themselves, fill in your target and wordlist once, and copy the result. Then read it back, piece by piece, so you know what you just ran. Everything happens in your browser — nothing is sent anywhere, and nothing is executed.

How to use commander

  1. 1 Pick a tool

    Choose one below. Tools with several modes — gobuster has seven — show them as buttons at the top; the mode decides which flags exist.

  2. 2 Choose your flags

    Hover any flag to see what it does, its default, and what it clashes with, before you pick it. A flag that cannot be combined with one you have already chosen is greyed out.

  3. 3 Fill in your values once

    Target, wordlist and anything else appear in the panel on the right as you need them. Flags you may give more than once, like -H, take one value per line.

  4. 4 Read it back, then copy

    The command is broken into labelled pieces underneath, so you can see what each part does. Copy stays disabled until the command would actually run.

Use the search box at the top to jump straight to a tool or a flag — typing recursion finds it in ffuf. Every tool also has a Manual tab with its real --help output, searchable, and the command to print it yourself.
